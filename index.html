Support workflow and response management

Customer support operations connected to volunteer, host, membership and application workflows, run in the CRM (HubSpot and Odoo).

Problem

Volunteers and hosts hit recurring questions and blockers across membership, profile verification, applications, host responses and stay logistics. Without a shared way to classify and route cases, the support team re-reads similar cases from scratch, answers vary, and users are left unsure what happens next.

Sizing assumption: a small set of recurring topics accounts for roughly 60 to 70% of incoming cases. To be validated against ticket history before build.

Users

  • Volunteers and hosts raising questions
  • Support team (Level 1)
  • Operations (Level 2) and product or tech (Level 3) escalation owners

Goals and targets

  • First response within 4 business hours
  • Resolve 70% of cases at Level 1
  • Cut repeat contacts on the same issue by 25% within two quarters
  • Every case has a category, owner and status

Primary workflow

  1. Issue received
  2. Classify
  3. Template or FAQ
  4. Resolve or escalate
  5. Close and learn

Functional requirements

IDRequirementPriority
FR-1Classify every case by topic (membership, verification, application, host response, stay logistics, other), user type, urgency (P1 to P3) and lifecycle stage.P0
FR-2Library of at least 25 approved response templates for the most frequent scenarios, selectable by scenario and editable before sending.P0
FR-3Escalation matrix with named owners: Level 1 support, Level 2 operations, Level 3 product or tech. Mandatory handoff note. Response targets of 4 hours, 1 business day and 3 business days.P0
FR-4Case record in the CRM with status, owner, timestamps, next action and resolution note.P0
FR-5Context panel showing the linked profile, application and membership status for the person who raised the case.P1
FR-6Support dashboard: open cases by status, ageing, escalations and top issue categories.P1
FR-7Auto-acknowledgement sent when a new case is logged.P2
FR-8Monthly review of the top 10 issue themes, feeding updates to FAQs and in-product copy.P2

Acceptance criteria

  • Given a new case, when it is saved, then category, owner and status are all filled in.
  • Given an escalation, then the receiving owner and next action are recorded on the case.
  • Given a recurring scenario, then a template can be picked, edited and sent in under one minute.
  • Given a resolved case, then a resolution note is stored and searchable for later review.

Success metrics

MetricHow it is measuredProposed target
First-response timeCRM timestamps, weekly4 business hours or less
Level 1 resolution rateCases closed without escalation70% or more
Repeat-contact rateSame user and issue category within 30 days25% lower than baseline
Escalation rateEscalated cases as a share of all cases20% or less
Ageing backlogCases open more than 5 days5% of open cases or less
Template usageLevel 1 replies that used a template60% or more
User satisfactionPost-resolution rating4.2 out of 5 or higher

Risks and mitigations

  • Templates can feel generic. Mitigation: keep them editable and review wording monthly.
  • Adoption depends on the team using the new fields. Mitigation: SOP, a short walkthrough and a weekly data-quality check.
  • Ticket history may be thin. Mitigation: run a 4-week manual tagging pass to set the baseline.

Release plan

  1. Phase 1Weeks 1 to 2

    Case taxonomy and escalation matrix agreed with support and operations.

  2. Phase 2Weeks 3 to 5

    CRM fields, template library and context panel built. UAT with the support team.

  3. Phase 3Weeks 6 to 8

    Dashboard, rollout, SOP handover and first baseline review.

Systematic test-taking operations

Operational coordination for planning, administering and tracking student assessments.

Contour Education

Problem

Running tests involves several people, schedules, instructions, completion records and follow-up actions. When these live in separate places, coordinators chase status by hand, readiness gaps are found late, and reschedules lose their reason. A consistent workflow makes each test's status easy to see and follow up.

Sizing assumption: about 150 students across 6 cohorts, with around 3 assessments per cohort each term. Exact tools, rules and volumes to be validated against the real process before treating this as a build specification.

Users

  • Students taking the test
  • Academic or operations coordinators
  • Test administrators and reviewers
  • Program and delivery stakeholders

Goals and targets

  • At least 95% of tests completed on the scheduled date
  • Reschedules at 5% of tests or fewer
  • Cut manual coordination time by 30%
  • Review and follow-up within 2 working days of completion

Primary workflow

  1. Prepare test
  2. Confirm readiness
  3. Schedule
  4. Conduct
  5. Record outcome
  6. Follow up

Functional requirements

IDRequirementPriority
FR-1Test record with assessment name, cohort or student, due date, duration, instructions and owner.P0
FR-2Readiness checklist (instructions shared, student confirmed, access and materials checked, administrator assigned), completed at least 2 working days before the test.P0
FR-3Status model: Draft, Ready, Scheduled, In Progress, Completed, Reviewed or Follow-up, with allowed transitions only.P0
FR-4Exception capture for non-completion, rescheduling and technical or operational issues. A reason code is mandatory.P0
FR-5Role-based access so only authorized users see student information and assessment records.P0
FR-6Reminders to students 48 and 24 hours before a test, and to owners when an action is overdue.P1
FR-7Operations view of tests in the next 14 days, overdue actions and outstanding reviews.P1
FR-8Weekly summary of completion, reschedules and pending reviews for program stakeholders.P2

Acceptance criteria

  • Every test shows an owner, a date and a current status.
  • Any test that is overdue or incomplete appears in an exception queue.
  • A reschedule keeps the reason and the new schedule on the record.
  • An authorized user can see the latest completion and review status for any test.

Success metrics

MetricHow it is measuredProposed target
On-time completion rateCompleted on scheduled date, per term95% or more
Missed or rescheduled testsShare of scheduled tests5% or less
Manual coordination timeCoordinator hours per week, before and after30% lower than baseline
Completion-to-review timeDays from Completed to Reviewed2 working days or less
Overdue actions clearedTime in the exception queueWithin 1 working day
Record completenessTests with owner, date, status100%

Risks and mitigations

  • Student data is sensitive. Mitigation: role-based access from day one.
  • Coordinators may keep side spreadsheets. Mitigation: pilot with one cohort and retire the old tracker once it works.
  • The real process may differ from this workflow. Mitigation: map it with coordinators before build.

Release plan

  1. Phase 1Weeks 1 to 2

    Map the current process, agree statuses and the readiness checklist.

  2. Phase 2Weeks 3 to 4

    Pilot with one cohort: test records, exception queue and reminders.

  3. Phase 3Weeks 5 to 8

    Roll out to all cohorts, add the operations view and weekly summary.

Shopify website development and customer journey

Product discovery, product detail pages, catalogue operations and purchase journey for a handloom saree brand, across 2,400+ SKUs.

Problem

Shoppers comparing handloom sarees need clear information on fabric, weave, colour, motifs and occasion, and a way to narrow a very large catalogue. Internal teams need dependable catalogue and promotion updates so website changes can be tested and released with fewer content or pricing mistakes, in step with marketplace listings.

Targets below are proposals. Baselines come from Shopify and GA4 data collected before the first release.

Users

  • Shoppers discovering and comparing sarees
  • E-commerce and catalogue operators
  • Marketing and merchandising teams
  • Developers and external vendors

Goals and targets

  • Product data complete on every listing before it is published
  • 10% relative increase in collection-to-product-view rate
  • 5% relative increase in checkout completion
  • 3 or fewer significant issues per release

Primary workflow

  1. Discover collection
  2. Compare product pages
  3. Select product
  4. Cart and checkout
  5. Order confirmation

Functional requirements

IDRequirementPriority
FR-1Navigation and collection taxonomy by product type, fabric, weave, colour and occasion. Any product reachable in 3 clicks or fewer from the home page.P0
FR-2Product page standard: name, fabric, weave and craft details, dimensions, care, price, availability, delivery information and at least 5 images including a weave close-up and a drape shot.P0
FR-3Variant, inventory and pricing data that matches the product record, kept consistent with Amazon, Myntra and AJIO listings.P0
FR-4Cart, checkout, payment, order confirmation and transactional messages validated end to end.P0
FR-5Responsive layouts tested at 360, 390, 768, 1024 and 1440 px widths.P0
FR-6Pre-release checklist covering content accuracy, broken links, availability, discount logic and purchase flow.P0
FR-7Filters for fabric, weave, colour, occasion, price band and availability.P1
FR-8Scheduled promotions with start and end dates, tested before activation.P1
FR-9Mobile Largest Contentful Paint of 2.5 seconds or less on collection and product pages.P1

Acceptance criteria

  • No product is published with a required field empty.
  • Each collection and filter link opens the expected set of products.
  • Price, variant and stock shown on the page match the product record.
  • A test order completes the agreed checkout path in UAT.
  • Discount rules match the approved campaign setup.

Success metrics

MetricHow it is measuredProposed target
Collection to product-view rateGA4, before and after each release10% higher than baseline
Add-to-cart rateGA4 product-page sessions10% higher than baseline
Checkout completion rateShopify analytics5% higher than baseline
Product-data completenessCatalogue audit of required fields100%
Issues after releaseSignificant defects logged within 7 days3 or fewer per release
Mobile page speedLargest Contentful Paint on key pages2.5 seconds or less
Filter usageCollection sessions that use a filter30% or more

Risks and mitigations

  • With 2,400+ SKUs, bad data spreads fast. Mitigation: required-field rules and a catalogue audit before each release.
  • Website and marketplace listings can drift apart. Mitigation: one source of truth for price and stock, checked in the pre-release list.
  • Vendor timelines can slip. Mitigation: ship in three phases with UAT after each.

Release plan

  1. Phase 1Weeks 1 to 3

    Taxonomy, navigation and the product page standard, applied first to top-selling collections.

  2. Phase 2Weeks 4 to 6

    Filters, variant and inventory rules, scheduled promotions.

  3. Phase 3Weeks 7 to 8

    Checkout validation, responsive testing, page speed work and release.
